Why Emergency Water Damage Restoration Is Not a DIY Job

In an emergency, the hard part is not mopping up what you can see. What takes skill is figuring out where everything else has soaked in. Baseboards, wall cavities and the space beneath flooring all take on water in minutes. Our team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find it, so the wet areas you cannot see are treated the same night instead of being found weeks later as a smell or a stain.

Our night crew arrives with gear homes simply do not have, including commercial air movers, properly sized dehumidifiers and truck-mounted extractors. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. When it comes time to file a claim, that documentation helps. From the first night to a dry house, a single crew stays on your job.

How to Tell You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Carpet, drywall or hardwood has been soaked for more than a few hours.
  • The water keeps running, and the shutoff valve is out of reach or nowhere to be found.
  • You came home to water and do not know how long it ran.
  • A water heater tank has failed and is emptying across the floor.
  • Water from a floor above is streaming down interior walls.
  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
  • Sewage has backed up, or the water is dark with a foul odor.

Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Every member of the household should know where the main water valve is and how it closes.
  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Keep the number of an emergency restoration company saved in your phone before you need it.
  • Know where your electrical panel is so power to wet rooms can be cut safely.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • Place leak sensors near the water heater, washing machine, dishwasher and under sinks.
