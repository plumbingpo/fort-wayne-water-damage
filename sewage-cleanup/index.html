Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Fort Wayne Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Fort Wayne, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

Hazard Check and Containment

Our team assesses the extent of sewage contamination, identifies safety hazards and establishes containment to prevent cross-contamination of unaffected areas.

We extract every bit of standing sewage. Contaminated porous materials including carpet, padding, drywall and insulation are removed and properly disposed of as biohazard waste.

We dry out any moisture that remains using commercial equipment. An industrial deodorizing treatment gets rid of the sewage smell. Finally, a clearance test verifies that people can safely use the area again.

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
